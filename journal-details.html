7 Materials DefiningModern Luxury Interiors

  • AuthorNisha D’Souza
  • Published14 September 2026
  • Reading time8 minutes

Luxury has moved on from gloss. The interiors we are asked for most today are quiet, tactile and slightly imperfect. Here are the seven materials we specify again and again, and why each earns its place.

Every project at Atelier Forma begins with a table of samples. Before we choose a sofa or a lamp, we choose the surfaces the room will be made of, because material sets the mood, the maintenance and the way a space ages. The list below is not a trend report. It is a working palette, built from more than a decade of specifying, installing and living with these materials.

1. Honed limestone

Limestone has a soft, matte face that catches light without glare. Honed rather than polished, it is forgiving underfoot and hides everyday wear. We use Kota and Jaisalmer stone for floors, wall cladding and courtyard edges, and always view the actual slabs before approval, because colour varies more than the sample suggests.

A material earns its place when it looks better after ten years of use than it did on the day it was installed.

Ananya Ramesh, creative director

2. Oiled timber

Oiled oak and teak bring warmth and grain to rooms that might otherwise feel severe. A hardwax oil finish keeps the wood breathing and can be refreshed in an afternoon. We avoid heavy lacquer, which flattens the surface and chips in ways that are difficult to repair.

3. Unlacquered brass

Brass is the warm accent in almost every project. Unlacquered, it develops a soft patina from touch, which makes handles and taps feel personal over time. We specify solid brass wherever hands land and use thin plate for inlays and shelving.

4. Lime plaster

Burnished or textured, lime plaster gives walls a depth that paint cannot. It is breathable, low in embodied carbon and gently reflective, so light seems to fall into it rather than bounce off. Plaster also repairs well: small marks can be blended in rather than repainted.

5. Fluted glass

Reeded glass softens views while letting light travel. We use it for pantry doors, shower screens and study partitions, where it provides privacy without closing a room down. Low-iron glass keeps the tone clear and avoids a green cast.

Fewer materials, used more honestly, always make the richer room.

Nisha D’Souza, materials lead

6. Terrazzo

Poured in place with chips of local marble, terrazzo offers the durability of stone with the character of a hand-made surface. It is a natural fit for kitchens, thresholds and bathrooms, and the chip size and colour can be tuned to each project.

7. Handmade tiles

Glazed clay tiles add colour and variation without artifice. Each one differs slightly in tone and edge, giving walls and floors a lively, crafted surface. We pair them with calmer materials so that they read as a moment, not a pattern.

The thread through all seven is honesty. Each material shows what it is, wears with grace and can be repaired. That is, to us, the truest definition of modern luxury.

  • Oiled timberTeak and oak, hardwax oil, 2 coats
